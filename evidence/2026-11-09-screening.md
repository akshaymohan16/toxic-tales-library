# Candidate screening for the 10–14 November 2026 batch

Six peer-reviewed candidates were located/screened. Screening is not full-text verification; only the two selected articles were verified for production. Older publication dates are displayed honestly; these are not advertised as new 2026 discoveries.

1. SELECT: Polverino et al. (2021), Psychoactive pollution suppresses individual differences in fish behaviour. DOI 10.1098/rspb.2020.2294. Replicated long-term design; mean-versus-variation lesson. Full-text claim review in tt-002.md.
2. SELECT: Michelangeli et al. (2024), Pharmaceutical Pollution Alters the Structure of Freshwater Communities and Hinders Their Recovery from a Fish Predator. DOI 10.1021/acs.est.4c02807. Recovery under continuing exposure; replicated outdoor mesocosms. Full-text review in tt-003.md.
3. PARK: Field-realistic antidepressant exposure disrupts group foraging dynamics in mosquitofish (2019), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6892513/ . Interesting social behaviour; full-text verification not completed in this batch.
4. PARK: Pharmaceutical pollution disrupts the behavior and predator-prey interactions of two widespread aquatic insects (2022), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9758520/ . Potential future taxonomic breadth; not approved from abstract alone.
5. CONTEXT/PARK: Long-term effects of widespread pharmaceutical pollution on trade-offs between behavioural, life-history and reproductive traits in fish, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11880659/ . Variation responses can differ across endpoints/designs. Needs a separate complete primary-source review before its own story.
6. CONTEXT/PARK: No effect of a widespread pharmaceutical pollutant on behavior, physiology, or morphology in guppies (2026), DOI 10.1093/beheco/arag080, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42568868/ . Null findings prevent a universal 'all fish traits are changed' narrative. Abstract checked; not treated as a direct replication of the two-year activity-variance study.

Only exact claims checked in the selected original papers enter published content. These two stories share a chemical but differ in organism level, question and design. Subsequent batches should broaden topics instead of automatically repeating pharmaceutical fish studies.
