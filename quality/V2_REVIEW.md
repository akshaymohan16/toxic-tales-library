# Independent review of Rebuild Preview v2

Decision: **DO NOT PUBLISH**. Date: 7 November 2026.
The owner delegated judgement to the editor and requires at least the quality of the pre-automation Toxic Tales account. The rejected generic TT-002 upload is not the benchmark. This record does not claim a complete comparison with originals that could not be retrieved.

## What was inspected
- The actual local 1080x1920, 40.083-second MP4: sampled frames across all seven scenes, not only the supplied storyboard.
- Exact narration text and segment timings from the generated voice artifact.
- Native and phone-sized views of the layout, text, captions and diagrams.
- Speech timing/engine metadata, not a completed auditory comparison. Do not relabel these checks as having listened to the voice.

## Editorial findings
1. Opening, 0–4.2 seconds: 'The average isn't the whole story' and 'Imagine measuring a whole group of fish...' are abstract. They withhold the actual organism/chemical finding. Lead with the specific biological change and an understandable question; do not substitute a statistics slogan for a story.
2. Pacing, 4.2–23.6 seconds: roughly 19 seconds rely primarily on three slide-like explanatory layouts. The result frame lasts about 8.5 seconds. Most motion is progress/caption changes and minor graphical motion. No universal cut-every-N-seconds rule is imposed, but a prolonged unchanged composition needs a clear explanatory purpose.
3. Visual variety: one aquarium clip and one guppy photograph recur, including successive late scenes. Enlarging the same photograph is not new story-specific visual coverage. The photo and twelve-tank schematic can be useful ingredients; their present assembly is not an accepted standard.
4. Redundant reading: persistent uppercase headline, smaller explanation, source/disclaimer microtext and separate subtitles compete for attention. Use one dominant idea, with the caption supporting rather than duplicating it.
5. Caption meaning, around 26 seconds: the standalone chunk 'a loss of every personality trait.' is separated from the negation in the previous chunk. This temporarily reverses the intended meaning for a silent viewer. The later population-decline sentence has a similar risk when negation and claim appear separately. Caption chunks must preserve negation, uncertainty and qualifiers with the claim; exact transcript fidelity alone is insufficient.
6. Scientific visual scope: coloured dot arrays are conceptual, but the tiny 'not measured data' label is weak protection. Do not present an invented point cloud as a quasi-result graphic. Use an unmistakably schematic explanation or traceably correct source data with readable provenance. Do not imply equal true means from a non-significant difference.
7. Story focus: a separate 'WILD POPULATION DECLINE?' scene introduces a claim the story need not make. Keep material scope limits, but express them in the story rather than spending a scene denying an invented sensational premise. The closing 'look beyond the average' repeats the opening instead of delivering a specific biological takeaway.
8. Narration: switching from eSpeak to Kokoro establishes a different synthesis method, not naturalness, pronunciation, expressiveness or parity with the owner's old videos. Those remain unverified and cannot receive a pass from a successful export or volume measurement.

## Replacement acceptance
The editor, not the owner, makes the quality decision. Require a clear organism-led hook, scene-specific visual coverage, explanations that develop rather than simply sit on screen, phrase captions that preserve scientific meaning, a full auditory check with an honest account of its scope, and no deterioration relative to accessible original references. Validate the entire repeatable build on two distinct stories before resuming. Keep the zero-additional-cost requirement, source verification, duplicate controls and publication records intact. Never let calendar quota override a failed quality review.
