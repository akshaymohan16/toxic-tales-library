# Toxic Tales visual pilot standard

Status: editorial/visual approach approved for integration on 7 November 2026; a NEW finished pilot is still required for owner approval. The rejected TT-003 films are not benchmarks. Do not claim a new export matches or exceeds the owner's earlier VEED.io work until the owner judges it. Read ../MEMORY.md and EDITORIAL_REVIEW_SCHEMA.md at the start of each session.

## Observed reference and changes
The earlier Desert Locust Upsurge Reel at https://www.instagram.com/toxic_tales_eco/reel/DKUQ1HUMjEB/ was visually inspected. It uses full-frame organism/environment footage and short, bold white italic captions with strong outlines and occasional colour emphasis. The pilot applies those useful treatments with the account's near-black and electric-lime identity. Its claims remain limited by the current paper records, regardless of the old Reel's claims.

The previous microscopy pipeline decoded landscape footage to 960 x 540, then vertically cropped and enlarged it. The new pipeline decodes at 24 fps and 1280 pixels high for the 720 x 1280 composition; portrait source footage starts at 2160 x 3840. Source-specific cache names prevent old frames from replacing new footage. Final delivery is H.264 1080 x 1920 with AAC audio. Output dimensions alone do not establish true image detail.

## Review the actual export
- Open with a clear organism/process and a comprehensible situation immediately; avoid a branding lead-in. Three seconds is a useful editing ambition, not an empirical guarantee. The opening image and words must work together for a viewer who has no previous context.
- Use legible captions with a strong outline, restrained lime emphasis and brief entrance motion. Preserve complete qualifications and negations; do not make the viewer chase animated words.
- Use full-screen, correctly identified reference imagery. Preserve the subject when cropping, and avoid over-enlargement, repeated blurry shots or a photo floating inside a large empty frame.
- Choose a paper and its explanatory visuals together before writing the script. A study without usable behaviour imagery or a clear, accurate diagrammatic explanation may be unsuitable for a Reel. Primary study footage is valuable when available and reusable; verified reference footage is not evidence of the experimental result.
- Use diagrams for relationships that footage cannot explain: who eats whom, movement between habitats, a route of exposure or a measured comparison. Mark conceptual illustrations; never fabricate numerical evidence. Do not reuse the rejected fish/tank design for unrelated subjects.
- Vary shots for meaning, not simply to satisfy a scene counter. The visual sequence should remain understandable without sound and narration should remain coherent without reading captions.
- Check all encoded scenes and cover at phone viewing size, including the start and end of animated transitions. Record sharpness, caption collisions, safe margins, pacing and any remaining limitations honestly.
- Pair every narration segment with a visual whose purpose is to identify, demonstrate, compare or explain. Use concrete scale, slow motion, arrows, selective highlighting and purposeful changes of view when they help understanding. Do not call decorative movement an explanation.
- Compare the next candidate with the exact owner-approved pilot once approval is recorded. A written review field or successful build cannot substitute for visual judgement.

The complete unpublished review package is generated at pilot.html. Its film, caption, cover, source record and exact file hashes must remain linked. Before rendering, record the per-segment visual plan and substantive visual review. After encode, an independent reviewer must inspect the actual output and complete the final editorial/visual review against the exact files. Any later visual or audio change requires a fresh export and review. Owner approval and the current hold are governed by automation/config.json and OPERATIONS.md.
