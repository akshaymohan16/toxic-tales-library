## Evidence and claim limits

1. **Stillness is not enough.** Portia fimbriata offspring studied in laboratory conditions. Characteristic resting posture beneath a silk platform, recurring bouts of retinal-tube movement and twitching. Main Results sleep behavior, Methods and Figs. 2/5. Ten spiders in the detailed sleep-architecture analysis (8 subadult, 2 adult); 109 REM-like bouts. Some quiet wakefulness may occur within the scored sleep window.
2. **Arousal:** 30 adult spiders tested once each in one of three states, 10 per state. Main Methods Arousal threshold and Results, Fig. 3: a 400 Hz sound ramp over 10 seconds. All awake and quiet-state spiders responded within the trial; four of ten in REM-like bouts did not. Those four responded when retested awake. Among responding spiders, model-estimated response latencies were longer in REM-like (4.27 s) versus awake (1.69 s), adjusted p<0.0001; REM-like vs quiescent comparison was not significant after adjustment (p=0.0548). Do not equate quiet-state animals with REM-like animals or claim within-animal comparisons of the three groups.
3. **Rebound:** Methods and Results sleep disruption/recovery, Fig. 4. Flies were introduced for a 12-hour disturbance; this was not demonstrated complete deprivation. Of 17 spiders tested, 13 had analyzable paired recordings. Mean REM-like bout count 6.1 to 10; mean total REM-like duration 9.4 to 16.9 minutes, +79.8%. Average bout duration did not increase significantly. These are group summaries, not individual paths. Undisturbed controls (n=5) did not show a similar sleep-window increase, but the film omits this control result rather than relying on appendix S2. The disturbance is not a manipulation solely of REM sleep, and other effects cannot be excluded.
4. **Conclusion:** Convergent behavioral evidence supports regulated sleep and recurring REM-like periods. Neural equivalence to vertebrate REM sleep or dreaming was not tested. No EEG or direct neural recording. Not universal proof for all spiders.
