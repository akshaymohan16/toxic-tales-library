## Question and design

Could a fish without a mammalian neocortex learn fine distinctions between human face images? A two-alternative forced-choice task used **Toxotes chatareus**, an archerfish able to hit targets above water with a jet. An LCD screen lay above and parallel to the water. Each correct spit earned one food pellet; an incorrect choice ended the trial. Fish already had some experience in spitting at monitor stimuli; prior experience varied.

**Experiment 1: four fish.** They first learned one rewarded face (CS+) and one unrewarded face (CS−). Two pairs of fish had the opposite trained face as CS−. Training sessions had 21–31 trials, with criterion at least 71% correct for two consecutive sessions. An intermediate step paired CS− with eight new rewarded face images. Testing then paired the **familiar unrewarded face with one new face at a time**, sampled from a pool of **44**. Ten original-pair trials and four intermediate faces served as reminders within each block and were excluded; each analysed block therefore contained 44 novel-face trials. Fish 1 and 4 completed four blocks, fish 2 and 3 two blocks. Repeated trials are not independent animals.

**Experiment 2: four different fish.** No intermediate training stage; different unrewarded faces assigned to different fish. Sessions had 30 trials; training criterion at least 75% correct twice consecutively. Test pool **18** new faces, again only two faces per trial. An original-pair reminder was excluded from each analysed block of 18 trials. All four fish completed four test blocks. An identical oval mask removed outside head-shape variation, all pictures were greyscale, and mean pixel brightness was normalized to 128. This controls those three candidate cues; it does not identify which remaining features the fish actually used.

Stimulus set: 62 frontal views of rendered three-dimensional human head models, all described in the Methods as Caucasian female faces, without hair or clothing. This restricted image set does not represent dynamic human encounters, all face populations, emotion recognition or recognising owners.

## Results and replication units

Both four-fish experiments supported above-chance discrimination of the learned negative face from new faces. Chance for this two-choice task is 50%. Experiment 1 GLMM Z=5.949, P<0.001. Experiment 2 GLMM Z=6.794, P<0.001. Fish ID and block were included as random factors. These are separate cohorts within one paper, not independent laboratory replications.

Reported test-block correct-choice means (percent ± SD):

| Block | Experiment 1 | Experiment 2 |

| 1 | 64.75 ± 8.342, four fish | 73.50 ± 12.77, four fish |

| 3 | 66.50 ± 2.121, two fish | 77.50 ± 6.351, four fish |

## Limits and reporting cautions

- Eight fish total across two separate four-fish experiments; trial totals cannot replace biological replication.
- Operant learning with food rewards and prior monitor-spitting experience, not spontaneous social recognition.
- Test asks fish to avoid one known unrewarded image while choosing new ones, not choose their owner among 44 people.
- Faces are shown in pairs. Forty-four and eighteen refer to novel-image pools, not simultaneous arrays or numbers of fish.
- Greyscale, normalized brightness and oval masks rule out only those cues. Local features, combinations and individual strategies remain unresolved.
- Original Discussion limits its claims to still frontal views. Later work on viewpoint changes exists (Newport et al. 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.anbehav.2018.09.002); therefore do not say no fish has ever been tested on rotations.
- The result does not establish that human faces need no specialized brain circuitry in humans, that fish understand identity or that owners are recognised.
- Source contains an internal Experiment 2 training-session inconsistency: text says 8–17 sessions but its individual list includes Fish 7 at seven. Source also describes the mixed-model link as logit in Results and log in Statistical analyses. Neither disputed detail appears in the narration or visual metrics. No attempt is made to silently resolve them.
