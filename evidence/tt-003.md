## Claim checks and source locations
- Methods 2.1: twelve 360-L outdoor mesocosms containing 270 L water; three exposure groups with four tanks each. Solvent control and two fluoxetine treatments. Tanks, not repeated water samples, are independent exposure units.
- Methods 2.2: 113 days, comprising establishment (50), fish introduction (21), fluoxetine introduction (21), and post-fish-removal (21). Each tank received five male eastern mosquitofish. All tanks experienced fish; there was no continuously fish-free comparison.
- Methods 2.2.3–2.2.4: treatment dosing continued after fish removal, to the experiment's end. Therefore this is recovery following predator removal under continuing exposure, not persistence after chemical exposure ceased.
- Methods 2.3.1: repeated zooplankton water samples, identification/counting blind to treatment. Results 4.3.1: copepod nauplii dominated; analysis concerns total abundance, not species diversity.
- Results 4.3.1, paragraphs 3–4: exposed-treatment abundance increased less than controls, with divergence primarily after fish removal. The authors explicitly report high uncertainty and variation among tanks/sampling weeks. We omit unstable percentage point estimates from the Reel.
- Results 4.3.1, paragraph 2: nutrients added before fish introduction prevent assigning the initial zooplankton decline solely to fish predation. This confound is retained in the caption/source note.
- Mechanism is unresolved. The story makes no claim that fluoxetine increased fish consumption, caused whole-lake collapse, or produced permanent post-exposure damage.
