## Clean frames without loss of scientific meaning

- Remove the small bottom source/method/species/production-note panel and decorative progress or timeline bar. Do not add persistent "Field notes", episode codes, technical metadata, production notices or other unnecessary microtext. This overrides earlier default footer treatments for future Reels.
- Keep readable speech captions and meaningful labels, units, counts, comparisons and qualifications that the viewer needs to understand the scientific story. Decisive limitations still belong in the narration and film; removing clutter is not permission to remove uncertainty or causal limits.
- Put full citations, DOI/full-text links, study/method detail, organism identification, asset author/source/licence/changes, reference-image provenance and production disclosures in the post caption and permanent research record. Retain claim-level evidence locations and all source records. Use a concise caption summary plus the linked record when the full detail is too long for Instagram.
- Choose and sequence reference imagery so viewers are not led to believe it records the actual experiment or an observed event it does not depict. Do not narrate "watch this happen" over unrelated footage, use a frost effect as evidence of freezing, or present conceptual drawings as measured data. The caption and research record must identify reference/conceptual assets. Where a particular scene would otherwise materially mislead, redesign it or use one brief, legible, scene-specific label integrated with the explanation; do not restore the rejected fine-print footer. Honour any asset licence that genuinely requires in-scene attribution, or choose another suitable asset.
- Keep the improved native-resolution composition, licensed font hierarchy, source-specific figures, purposeful motion and designed companion carousels. Do not trade useful visuals for decorative branding. Carousel source pages and meaningful scientific labels remain allowed; the owner's Reel-footer instruction is not a ban on readable citations in a companion research carousel.
