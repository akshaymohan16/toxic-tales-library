# Toxic Tales: science journalism production standard
Standard `story-first-v1`, approved for workflow integration by the owner on 7 November 2026 after rejecting both the experiment/results summary and the later “The fish were gone” pilot. Read ../MEMORY.md and EDITORIAL_REVIEW_SCHEMA.md at every session start. This specification is mandatory for new films and supersedes the old abstract-style approach. Accuracy remains necessary; it does not by itself make a compelling story. Approval of the approach is not approval of a finished film. Publishing remains held for the new pilot review.

## Reporting and craft research
The reporting process examined these journalism training sources. Their lessons are applied as editorial choices, not as a claim of formal training or credentials.
- The Open Notebook, [Serving Up Snippets of Science in Short Videos](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2022/03/22/serving-up-snippets-of-science-in-short-videos/): plan words and visual evidence together; make the opening intelligible and visually active; keep spoken clauses short.
- The Open Notebook, [Nailing the Nut Graf](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2014/04/29/nailing-the-nut-graf/): establish early what the story means and why an unfamiliar viewer should care.
- MIT Knight Science Journalism, [Building Narrative Into the Reporting Process](https://ksjhandbook.org/editing-for-story/building-narrative-into-the-reporting-process/): reporting must look beyond the result for context, implications and unresolved questions; a convenient account still needs corroboration.
- MIT Knight Science Journalism, [Finding Structure Without Losing Touch With Science](https://ksjhandbook.org/editing-for-story/finding-structure-without-losing-touch-with-science/): scientific uncertainty belongs inside the narrative; avoid forcing a lone-hero, definitive-breakthrough or universal-solution story.
- Science Media Centre, [Best Practice Guidelines](https://www.sciencemediacentre.co.nz/coveringscience/best-practice-guidelines-for-reporting-on-science/): identify study scope, distinguish causation and interpretation, support context and avoid misleading headlines.
- The Open Notebook, [Joss Fong black-hole videogram](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2020/01/07/videogram-how-a-vox-video-explains-the-science-behind-the-first-photo-of-a-black-hole/): establish the conceptual difficulty so the result has meaning, and leave time to understand transitions.
- NASW, [Show, not tell: the art of writing science videos](https://www.nasw.org/article/show-not-tell-art-writing-science-videos): determine whether usable visuals exist and write around them; retain a separate factual check.

The subsequent comparative content review covered Deep Look/KQED, MinuteEarth, BBC Earth, Animalogic, Science News and OPB. Full transcripts were read where available; official descriptions/reporting and selected native Instagram/YouTube frames and captions were inspected. Access limits and exact examples are preserved in ../MEMORY.md. This was an editorial comparison, not private-analytics access or a controlled retention study.

Deep Look's caddisfly film establishes the larva's problem, follows visible building actions, explains the mechanism and returns to survival in the stream. MinuteEarth's mountain-lake fish premise is intelligible without prior context. Science News uses research footage to present and explain a puzzling movement. OPB's mussel story changes the interpretation of apparently abundant animals by investigating their ages. BBC's animal sequences establish clear actors and stakes before suspense. These are useful models of coherent explanation.

Animalogic's 89-second lynx Short is a counterexample for this project: its school premise is followed by substantial trait exposition rather than the promised training explanation. A teaser may serve a longer video, but the owner's Reel must provide a complete payoff. The reviewed KQED Instagram bat opening demonstrates the value of a visible, identifiable animal. Borrow useful craft selectively, not every dramatic title, biological simplification or implied animal thought. KQED's own [title-framing experiments](https://www.kqed.org/about/16163/cracking-the-code-deep-look-titles) were inconclusive across settings; do not promise a viral hook formula.

## Mandatory story brief before rendering
Select the question, published evidence and usable visual material together. A sound paper may be unsuitable for a Reel. Look for a behaviour, relationship, contradiction, dependency or genuine mystery; do not manufacture a public misconception or force human-health, catastrophe or pollution stakes. Fascination and ecological consequences are legitimate reasons to care.

Create editorial_story with question, why_it_matters, narrative_arc, context_sources and closing_payoff, plus the mandatory audience, subject, setting, opening_context, normal_expectation, story_selection, decisive_limit and per-segment visual_plan in EDITORIAL_REVIEW_SCHEMA.md. Each Reel should explain a full ecological story at an appropriate scale:
1. A visible organism, process or concrete puzzle with an intelligible subject and situation. The viewer must not need a caption or earlier post to decode the opening.
2. The relevant ecological relationship and stakes in plain words.
3. Separately checked real-world context: how the pressure arises, why the process matters, or what prior evidence shows.
4. A central question that the focal study can actually address.
5. Only the experimental detail necessary to understand the evidence.
6. The discovery and its interpretation, with the decisive limitation where it changes the meaning.
7. A bounded answer that fulfils the opening promise within this Reel. If the scientific answer remains incomplete, explain what the evidence changes and exactly what remains unresolved.

These are narrative functions, not seven mandatory title cards. A different story structure is welcome when it works better. Avoid adding generic background to an unchanged methods/results script. Let the organisms and ecological relationships carry the story; researchers need not be characters.

Read the original focal published full text and separately verify each contextual claim from published evidence. Use the sources the claims require, with no arbitrary minimum number that encourages filler. Record what was actually accessed. Do not invent interviews, quotes, researcher feelings, field scenes, precise mechanisms or measurements. Explain evidence conflicts or uncertainty when material. Distinguish general context, study findings, proposed mechanisms and our interpretation. A popular media example is a craft reference, not automatically scientific evidence for a new Reel.

The six pre-render review checks are mandatory and source-hashed. They require specific observations and supporting lines/shots/claim locations, not checkboxes. The reviewer must be able to retell the ecological insight in one plain sentence and the important limitation in another. A failed check requires revision before rendering.

## Film and visual production
The planning range is 60-85 seconds and the technical envelope 45-95 seconds. These are production choices, not scientifically established retention rules. Length serves comprehension. Do not fill time, race through narration or cut essential explanation to hit an arbitrary short runtime. New episodes use production.editorial_style=science-journalism and production.editorial_standard=story-first-v1. Legacy sources may remain archived, but every rejected TT-003 export remains an unsuitable quality benchmark.

Use a paired narration/visual storyboard. Most screen time should show relevant real organisms or meaningful motion, with focused diagrams where they explain relationships. Use actual research footage only with permission/licence and accurate identification; label reference imagery. Keep count labels, methods and sources secondary. Do not create several slide-like cards to stand in for a story. Do not imply numerical data through invented plot shapes or population counts. The viewer should grasp the ecological relationship from the visuals and follow a coherent story from the narration.

Keep phone-safe captions large and short; preserve negations. Open on an organism/process and move promptly to the relevant other organism or relationship. End with the story's takeaway/question and a compact source credit, not a generic branding slogan.

## Narrative release gate
Technical QA and exact hashes still apply. In addition to narrative_review, new films require a separate final_editorial_review after encoding. An independent reviewer must assess the actual export, including its opening, every shot, transitions, captions, ending and cover. Review playback when available, and report inspection limits honestly. Tie the review to the current editorial source hash, full fingerprint, evidence hash and every encoded file hash. Answer all of these before release:
- Does the opening image and narration establish the subject and situation immediately for a new viewer?
- Can the editor state the single question and what changes in the viewer's understanding?
- Is the ecological relationship explained before its experimental disruption?
- Is independently verified context connected naturally to the focal finding?
- Do the visuals explain the relationships and changes rather than repeat paragraphs?
- Is the pivotal caveat integrated where interpretation turns?
- Does the ending pay off the opening without overclaiming?
- Is the narration coherent on its own and the visual sequence intelligible on its own?
- Are any scenes, reactions, quotes, quantities, mechanisms or certainty unsupported?
- Can the reviewer retell both the ecological insight and important limitation without relying on the author's explanation?

A failed answer requires revision and a new export. Do not copy a pre-render judgement as if it described the encoded result. The programmatic gates validate record completeness and identity, not the truth of a review, reviewer independence, comprehension or audience interest. Do not call the result excellent solely because it passes tests. Do not claim listening without actual listening capability. Retain candid ASR/loudness evidence and actual encoded-frame review. After approval, learn from available account retention/watch-time, completion, saves, shares and substantive comments; avoid attributing success to one technique without evidence.

## Weekly automation
The eventual target remains two Reels from distinct papers plus one companion carousel each week, with source records, cost limit, scope restrictions and duplicate protection. The carousel may explain methods or limits; Reels need a complete story. Producer and publisher read this file and ../MEMORY.md before every run. Routine decisions become delegated after explicit resumption. The current owner-review hold overrides that authority: the new exact pilot must first be shown with cover, caption and research entry and approved by the owner. Do not publish a weak or failed story to fill a slot.
