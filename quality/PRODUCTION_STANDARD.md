## Scientific checks before rendering

Read the full peer-reviewed original paper. Record title, authors, DOI, journal, year, full-text URL, date checked, exact source locations for each spoken or visual claim, independent replication, controls, species, exposure and measured versus nominal doses, results, uncertainty and causal limits. Check corrections/retractions and relevant subsequent contrary findings. Abstracts, news stories and generated summaries are discovery aids, never sufficient proof. An unavailable or ambiguous paper is replaced with another fully checked candidate. Never invent numbers, data points, research footage or mechanistic certainty.

Report the context as carefully as the focal result. Use at least two separately verified published context sources where contextual claims are made. Create an `editorial_story` brief with question, why_it_matters, narrative_arc, context_sources and closing_payoff. Distinguish established context, study findings and the editor's interpretation. Do not invent interviews, quotations, researcher reactions or field scenes.

Maintain `evidence/<id>.md`, the episode JSON, screening notes and a dated batch record. Each entry publishes a searchable paper record and a source/credit page. Link the DOI and archive in each caption. Store legal media credits, licence URLs, source URLs, modifications and download hashes. Do not redistribute publisher PDFs or assume that an image on a journal page is reusable.

## Editorial acceptance

The visual reference is the owner's original account, including the 31 May 2025 desert-locust Reel (`DKUQ1HUMjEB`), viewed in the 7 November integration session: organism-first opening, nature imagery, short shots, prominent readable captions. The rejected eSpeak TT-002 post, v2 slideshow and TT-003 v4 methods/results film are never quality benchmarks. Original audio parity was not established and must not be claimed.

Tell a complete, contextual ecological story: a concrete puzzle, the relevant relationship and stakes, verified real-world context, a question, the evidence that addresses it, its decisive limitation and a bounded closing payoff. The study belongs inside that story. Generic methods/results summaries, including longer versions padded with background, fail editorial review. Methods-focused companion carousels remain appropriate.

The opening should show an organism/process with an intelligible, supported hook and no introductory logo card. Aim for 60-85 seconds; the technical envelope is 45-95 seconds. Use a paired narration/visual storyboard and enough time to understand each turn. Most screen time should show relevant organisms or meaningful motion; use focused diagrams to explain relationships. Avoid long repeated stock sequences and successive slide-like cards. More than a token camera zoom is needed to call a scene animated. Main captions must fit within the phone safe region at a readable size. Keep count labels, methods and citations secondary; avoid redundant headline, paragraph and subtitles. Keep a scientific negation with the claim it qualifies. Explicitly label conceptual drawings; avoid invented data-like graphs or population counts. Integrate the material caveat where it changes interpretation. End by answering or sharpening the opening question, with a compact source credit rather than a generic branding slogan.

Every completed export requires actual encoded-frame inspection at each shot, all carousel pages and the cover. Inspect a moving playback when available; compare comprehension, pacing, topic-specific visuals and readability, not just technical success. Read the final ASR transcript and listen when listening capability exists. Never claim human listening from a volume or transcript check. The technical audio gate uses segment and final-export transcription, critical terms, speech/caption matching, approximately -16 LUFS and safe true peak. Pin the accepted voice and retain the transcript/loudness evidence. A material pronunciation or visual problem requires correction and a new review.
