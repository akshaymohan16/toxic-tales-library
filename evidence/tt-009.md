Six experimental conditions: (1) active ensnared same-colony `C. cursor`, (2) active ensnared different-colony `C. cursor`, (3) active ensnared other-species `Camponotus aethiops`, (4) ensnared larval cricket, (5) chilled motionless ensnared nestmate, (6) empty nylon snare. Each condition had three tests from each of three lab colonies, nine group-level replicates per condition and 54 group trials altogether. Five worker ants participated per trial; the group of five, **not each ant**, was the experimental statistical unit. Individual subject ants were not reused. One test lasted seven minutes. The nylon thread loop attached the victim's waist to an approximately one-centimetre paper disc concealed beneath sand. The head and thorax remained above the sand.

## Claim-level evidence, exact scope

1. **The nylon restrained a partly buried ant in an artificial laboratory test.** Source: Results opening (six conditions and experimental apparatus); Materials and Methods (nylon pedicel loop, paper disc, sand, seven-minute trials). The diagram is a simplification, not actual experimental footage or a natural field observation.
2. **Workers sometimes dug, carried sand away, pulled limbs and bit the nylon snare itself.** Source: Results paragraph beginning "Across the 54 different tests"; Discussion paragraph "In sum." The paper does not report that every thread was cut or that every victim escaped. Do not add those outcomes.
3. **Rescue behaviors appeared in all nine group tests with active colony-mates and in none of the other 45 group tests.** Source: Results paragraph "Across the 54 different tests." Verbatim source wording: "in each of the 9 independent homocolonial tests" and "never was observed in any of the remaining 45 tests." These are observational **trial counts** under one controlled setup, not population proportions, individual worker sample sizes, or an estimate of wild rescue frequency. Overall group comparisons and adjusted pairwise results are in the same Results section, but the story does not claim every individual behavior differed significantly from every other condition.
4. **Only one to three of five potential rescuers performed rescue behaviors in positive tests.** Source: Materials and Methods paragraph "Although, as described above, we used a group of 5 ants". Do not say all five ants helped or all observed ants independently replicated the effect.
5. **The tests support discrimination among active colony-mates and several controls, not empathy or the mechanism of recognition.** Source: Results control outcomes and Discussion on pheromone/releaser hypotheses. A chemical cue is proposed, not directly established; intentional understanding and emotional states were not tested.
6. **The precise snare bite is notable but does not establish a natural rescue rate.** Source: Results and Discussion; original authors propose rescue in collapsed sand or antlion contexts, but manipulated nylon snare and confined group are laboratory constructs.
