## Visual direction

Aim for a coherent short natural-history film: a recognisable organism in a real setting, a legible question, close observation, a precise explanation of an otherwise invisible process, and a return to the animal with a bounded payoff. Use the account's green identity with a restrained palette chosen for the subject. A winter story may use dark forest greens, pale ice blues and a single warm accent; another ecology story needs its own meaningful palette and diagrams.

Popular-channel references guide craft, not factual claims or asset rights. Adopt specific useful choices such as a clear animal subject, purposeful changes of scale, uncluttered explanatory drawings and consistent visual hierarchy. Do not copy a channel's branding, characters, footage, illustrations, soundtrack or a supposed universal viral formula. Record whether each reference was actually viewed as a still, selected frames, playback or only a description.

## Typography and composition requirements

- Compose new Reel artwork at native 1080 by 1920, and carousel artwork at 1080 by 1350 or another deliberately chosen supported portrait format. An upscaled low-resolution design does not establish native detail.
- Use at most two compatible, properly licensed font families. Give headlines, spoken captions, diagram labels and source notes distinct roles through size, weight and spacing. Do not make every line a heavy all-capital headline.
- Use a distinctive but readable display face for a short opening promise and a highly legible sans serif for captions and scientific labels. Obtain fonts from their official repositories or another documented legitimate source, archive the licence and pin the actual files or download hashes.
- Keep the organism or explanatory relationship dominant. Position type away from the animal's eye/body and important figure features. Avoid a large empty frame around a small image and several competing blocks that repeat the same sentence.
- Measure line width with the actual font at the final size. Inspect the crop and cover at phone scale. Test both the portrait film and the narrower profile-grid crop. Shortening a phrase is preferable to making essential text tiny.
- Keep essential text away from the bottom caption region and right-side controls. Verify actual composition rather than assuming a pixel margin alone makes it safe. Scientific qualifications and negations must remain with their claims.

## Images, diagrams and motion

- Select high-resolution, correctly identified and legally reusable organism imagery with useful focal points before finalising the script. Inspect the source itself. Label reference imagery honestly and keep author, source-page, licence, modifications and download-hash records.
- Never use another species, a staged scene, generated imagery or a colour treatment as evidence of the studied biological event. In particular, a frost overlay on an ordinary frog photograph is not footage of a frog freezing. A conceptual animation must be identified as such.
- Draw scientific relationships specifically for the source paper. Use clean line work, consistent labels, deliberate scale changes, restrained colour coding and visible arrows only where they explain a supported direction or interaction.
- Separate a conceptual drawing from measured data. Do not invent curves, fractions, ice volumes, molecular counts, anatomical detail or survival percentages. Small drawn symbols must not imply that their number is a measured population size unless it is.
- Animate the explanatory process itself: a route, compartment, before/after contrast, movement or selective change supported by the evidence. A pan or zoom over a photograph can connect scenes, but it is not by itself an animated scientific explanation.
- Use motion and cuts to reveal one idea at a time. Avoid constant decorative particles, bouncing text and abrupt template transitions that compete with the narration. Hold decisive evidence and the final qualification long enough to understand them.
- Preserve sufficient original-image detail through the portrait crop; sharpening and output dimensions cannot recover missing information. Replace a poor asset or redesign its composition rather than conceal blur behind titles.

## Companion carousels

Create a designed companion rather than exported video frames. Use five to eight purposeful pages: a concrete cover promise, the setting/question, the useful mechanism or comparison, the decisive evidence, the essential limitation, and the source/next step as appropriate. These are functions, not a compulsory six-card sequence.

Each page has one main idea, one clear headline and an explanatory image or figure. Use a consistent grid, generous spacing, repeated label/colour meanings, unobtrusive page numbers and a readable source line. Keep dense methods in the research record; a carousel can clarify a method or caveat without becoming a wall of text. Its interpretation must match the Reel and paper, and all pages need their own final inspection.
