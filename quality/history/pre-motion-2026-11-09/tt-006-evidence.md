**Claim locations and boundaries**
1. The freshwater spider *Argyroneta aquatica* constructs silk-supported underwater air bells, carrying air from the surface. Introduction pp.2175–2176 and Results pp.2177–2178; Fig.1. It breathes atmospheric gas in the bell, not water directly with anatomical gills.
2. Oxygen in surrounding water can cross the bell's water/air boundary: paper Summary p.2175, Results pp.2178–2179 (gas conductance) and Discussion p.2179. Do not say oxygen is manufactured by the web.
3. Researchers measured bell volume, internal and adjacent O2 partial pressures with micro-optodes, and the resting spiders' respiration by respirometry. Methods pp.2176–2177. Fourteen **empty bell samples from eleven spiders** are non-independent bell measurements; metabolic tests also involved other spiders.
4. Under warm (~25°C) largely stagnant aquarium conditions, many voluntarily built bells could meet *resting* oxygen demand via diffusion. Discussion p.2179 and Conclusion p.2181. A few small bells did not; active and reproductive energetic requirements were not resolved. This is a resting-metabolism result, not demonstrated indefinite self-sufficient breathing for all spiders.
5. The bell slowly loses nitrogen and shrinks, so it still requires eventual replenishment. Discussion pp.2180–2181 and model Fig.6. The “more than a day” forecast is model-dependent, not an individually timed wild dive.
6. The spider's thin body air layer alone was insufficient to support its oxygen demand; larger bell surface mattered. Discussion p.2179 and Fig.1. Avoid claims about gills on legs or water-breathing skin.
7. Air renewal occurred particularly under low aquatic oxygen and small bells; previous aquarium reports described more frequent resurfacing under other circumstances. Discussion pp.2179–2181. No general statement that they *never* surface.

**Scope:** Laboratory physiology of a field-collected spider, not field survival, wild population abundance or a randomised ecological manipulation. Bell shapes and sizes vary; brief physical-gill cartoon arrows depict gas movement qualitatively, not measured rates or mole counts. Do not show an oxygen trace or invented exact-duration metric. Make the decisive limit spoken: resting spiders in this study, occasional air replacement still needed. Film's sole question is how air in an underwater bell stays breathable.
