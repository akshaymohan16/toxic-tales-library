# TT-007 — Bumblebees and wooden balls

**Scientific claims**
1. Commercial *Bombus terrestris audax* bumblebees spontaneously rolled 15-mm inedible wooden balls in a laboratory. Results Experiment 1 pp.243–244 and Fig.2; animal identification and colonies in Methods pp.240–241. The wooden balls were not flowers or food rewards.
2. In Experiment 1, 45 tagged bees performed 910 ball-rolling actions. Results p.243. These are **910 actions, not 910 independent bees**, and all from a laboratory colony in this experiment.
3. Bees could take a direct unobstructed path to food, without touching balls; objects were placed in side areas with mobile or immobilised balls. Methods p.241–242, Fig.1. Balls were not necessary to obtain nectar, pollen or shelter. Activity was repeated even after reaching feeding area, Results p.243.
4. Younger bees rolled balls more often than older bees in a separate age/novelty test (11/13 young versus 14/33 older rolled, greater actions per bee), Experiment 2, Results p.245, Fig.8. This supports age-related behavior, not a claim all young bees play.
5. In a distinct colour-chamber preference test with 42 female bees from four colonies, a colour previously paired with movable balls influenced later choice even when both chambers had no balls. Methods pp.242–243 Figs.3–4, Results p.246 and Fig.10; P=0.026. Other colour biases existed; this is association and a behavioral proxy for intrinsic reward, not direct mind-reading.
6. The study interpreted rolling as meeting operational **play-like behaviour criteria**: no obvious immediate function, repeated flexible actions, and apparent rewarding character. Intro pp.239–240 and Discussion pp.246–251. Wild prevalence, subjective enjoyment, benefit for future flower handling and sentience are NOT established. The authors explicitly note laboratory-stimulation alternative and need for wild studies, Discussion p.250.
7. Do not confuse this study with an earlier ball-rolling-for-sugar task (Loukola et al. 2017) referenced in Intro: in that earlier task balls were trained for reward, while the focal 2022 experiment made the activity unrewarded by food.

**Narrative limit**: A lab study found play-like rolling of wooden balls by bumblebees without food reward, with evidence of preference and age pattern. Whether an insect actually experiences fun, and whether wild bees do this, cannot be established directly. Spoken caveat must remain.
