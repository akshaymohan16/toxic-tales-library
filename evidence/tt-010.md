## Design and reported observations

Six temporarily captive wild-caught New Caledonian crows from Grande-Terre were trained to drop stones into a tube to release a platform reward BEFORE the displacement experiments. Dropping stones into water to raise floating food was not reported as a spontaneous wild discovery. Birds received 20 trials per task; six birds did Tasks 1–2, five did Tasks 3–4, and four did Tasks 5–6 because two stopped for reduced motivation. Test order was fixed, leaving possible carryover. Choice measures were the actual selected objects or tube, not independent birds for each drop.

1. **Water versus sand.** Across the first 20-trial task, 76.3% of stone drops entered the water-filled tube rather than sand; only water displaced upward to move a floating food reward. Source: Methods, Experiment 1 and Results, Experiment 1, lines 62–63 and 77–78 in cached main text.
2. **Sinking versus floating.** 88.0% of selected objects entered the water tube as sinking rather than floating objects. Source: Methods and Results, Experiment 2, lines 64–65 and 82–83. A floating nonfunctional object did not raise the reward.
3. **Solid versus hollow.** Birds selected solid rather than hollow objects in 89.0% of drops, with equal weights and sizes but different displaced water volumes. Source: Methods and Results, Experiment 3, lines 66–67 and 84–85.
4. **High starting water.** Across 20 trials, 86.8% of drops entered the higher-water wide tube. The preference developed over early trials; all four initially used the nonfunctional low tube on Trial 1. Source: Methods and Results, Experiment 5, lines 70–71 and 91–92.
5. **Two important failures.** Narrow tubes were not reliably favored on the first drop per trial (narrow chosen 56%, p=0.27); the same five birds often used wide tubes until the food rose. With a concealed U-tube connection, four birds chose the functional connected tube on average 48.9% of drops (p=0.79), near chance. Source: Results, Experiments 4 and 6, lines 86–94; Discussion, lines 99–103.

## What the evidence does and does not establish

The supported conclusion is **sensitivity to some functional object and tube properties with clear failures on others**, not a complete or species-wide understanding of hidden physics. Six trained subjects and only four completing all tasks limit generalization; prior exposure and fixed order can affect results. The paper itself notes that the narrow-tube test offered a workable reward from either tube, potentially weakening motivation to favor efficiency; U-tube visual causal connections were concealed. Choices within repeated trials are not six independent replicates per drop.
